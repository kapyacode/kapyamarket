If you want to start a blog, freelance or build an online business in South Africa, there is a problem nobody mentions in the glossy "make money online" guides: data costs real money, and the power does not always stay on. Many beginners stop before they begin because they think they need fast fibre and a big budget.

You do not. Most online work is mostly text, and text uses very little data. This guide shows how to organise your work so a small mobile data bundle goes a long way, and how to keep going when load-shedding hits.

## Why online work doesn't have to use much data

Writing a blog post, sending an email or updating a website uses a tiny amount of data compared with streaming video or scrolling social media. The data hogs are usually:

- Video streaming and video calls
- Social media feeds that auto-play videos
- App and Windows updates running in the background
- Large images and file uploads
- Cloud backups syncing without you noticing

Once you control these, most of your bundle is left for actual work.

## Step 1: Find out where your data is going

Before you change anything, look at what is using it.

- **On Android:** go to **Settings → Network & internet → Data usage** and look at which apps use the most.
- **On Windows:** go to **Settings → Network & internet → Data usage** to see usage per app.

Most people are surprised. A single app updating in the background can use more than a whole day of writing.

## Step 2: Stop the silent data drains

These settings take a few minutes and often save the most.

1. **Turn on Data Saver** on your phone (in the network settings on most Android phones).
2. **Set your connection as "metered" on Windows:** go to **Settings → Network & internet**, open your Wi-Fi or hotspot connection, and switch on **Metered connection**. This tells Windows to hold back large downloads.
3. **Turn off auto-updates over mobile data** in the Google Play Store, and update apps only when you are on Wi-Fi.
4. **Stop video auto-play** in social media apps (it is usually in each app's settings).
5. **Pause cloud syncing** (OneDrive, Google Drive and similar) while you are on a small bundle.

## Step 3: Work offline, connect only when needed

The cheapest way to work online is to be offline most of the time.

- **Write offline.** Draft your articles in Notepad, a Markdown editor or a document app, and connect only to publish or send.
- **Prepare images before uploading.** Large photos eat data. Shrink and compress them first, and use modern formats like WebP when you can.
- **Batch your online tasks.** Make a list of what needs the internet (publishing, checking email, replying to clients) and do it in one short session instead of staying connected all day.
- **Check email in short bursts.** Constantly refreshing a webmail page uses more data than most people expect.

If you use a static site tool like Hugo, this approach works well: writing and building the site happens on your computer, and publishing is a small upload.

## Step 4: Be smart with video and calls

Video is the biggest data cost.

- Watch tutorials at a lower quality (360p or 480p is enough for most how-to videos).
- Download videos or courses over Wi-Fi when you can, then watch them offline.
- For client calls, switch off your camera when it is not needed, or use voice only.
- Use text where it works. Many clients are happy with email or chat.

## Step 5: Find cheaper ways to connect

Prices and offers change often, so check with your own provider, but it is worth looking at:

- **Night or off-peak bundles.** Many networks offer cheaper data at certain hours. Schedule uploads and downloads for then.
- **Prepaid bundles with a data-per-rand focus.** Compare a few providers each time you top up, instead of always buying the same one.
- **Public Wi-Fi** at libraries, universities or other places where it is available. Avoid logging into banking or important accounts on public networks.
- **Phone hotspot only when needed.** A laptop on a hotspot can use data very quickly, so connect it only for the tasks that need it.

## Step 6: Plan for load-shedding

Power cuts interrupt work and can also drain your data when devices reconnect again and again. A simple plan helps:

1. **Know the schedule.** Use a load-shedding schedule app or your municipality's information so you can plan work around outages.
2. **Charge everything before the cut.** Laptop, phone and power bank should be full before the outage starts.
3. **Keep a power bank** for your phone, since the phone is also your internet.
4. **Protect your router.** If you have home Wi-Fi, a small backup power unit for the router can keep you online during short outages.
5. **Do offline work during outages.** Save writing, planning and editing for these times, and keep publishing for when the power and connection are back.
6. **Save often.** Use auto-save, so a sudden cut doesn't cost you an hour of work.

## A simple weekly routine

Here is one way to put it all together:

- **Offline time:** write, edit, plan content and design images.
- **Short online session once or twice a day:** publish, reply to messages, check stats.
- **Wi-Fi or off-peak time:** large downloads, updates and uploads.

## Final thoughts

Limited data and unreliable power are real problems, but they do not stop you from earning online. Work offline, connect briefly and on purpose, and avoid the background data drains. Start with one or two changes this week, and check your data usage again to see the difference.
