<ul>

<h2>Three things South Africans need to know first</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Shopify Payments is not available in South Africa.</strong> You need a third-party payment gateway, covered in Step 5.</li>
  <li><strong>Plan prices are listed in US dollars.</strong> Convert them at the current rate, and check whether your bank adds foreign-currency fees to the monthly charge.</li>
  <li><strong>Third-party gateways add an extra Shopify fee</strong> on every sale, on top of what the gateway charges you.</li>

<h3>Step 3: Add your products</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open <strong>Products</strong> in your dashboard and click <strong>Add product</strong>.</li>
  <li>Add the product name and a clear description.</li>
  <li>Upload good photos. Three to five per product works well.</li>
  <li>Set the price in rands and enter your stock quantity.</li>
  <li>Save the product.</li>
</ol>

<p><strong>Tips for better product descriptions:</strong></p>
<ul>
  <li>Lead with the benefit, then list the features.</li>
  <li>Include size, material and care details where relevant.</li>
  <li>Use words your customers would actually search for.</li>
  <li>Be honest. Accurate descriptions mean fewer returns.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Example (sample text only, replace it with your real product details):</strong></p>
<blockquote>
  <p><strong>Everyday Cotton T-Shirt, Unisex</strong></p>
  <p>A soft, breathable t-shirt for everyday wear. Available in sizes S to XXL and in several colours. Machine washable.</p>
  <p><strong>Price:</strong> R299. Free delivery in Gauteng on orders over R1,000.</p>
</blockquote>

<h3>Step 4: Connect a South African payment gateway</h3>

<figure style="margin: 24px 0px; text-align: center;">
  <img alt="Shopify Payments is not available in South Africa, so use a local payment gateway such as PayFast, Peach Payments, Ozow or Netcash" src="/images/blog/2ce13368051f.png" style="border-radius: 8px; height: auto; max-width: 100%;" />
  <figcaption style="color: #607080; font-size: 90%;">Third-party gateways add an extra Shopify transaction fee: 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow and 0.6% on Advanced.</figcaption>
</figure>
<p>This is the step where many South African beginners get stuck, because the tutorials they watch assume Shopify Payments is available. It is not available in South Africa, so you pick a local provider instead. The ones most often used with Shopify here are:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>PayFast or PayGate</strong> for card payments and Instant EFT</li>
  <li><strong>Peach Payments</strong> for cards, Instant EFT and digital wallets</li>
  <li><strong>Ozow</strong> for Instant EFT bank payments</li>
  <li><strong>Netcash</strong> for cards and Instant EFT</li>
  <li><strong>PayPal</strong> if you also sell to international customers</li>
</ul>

<p>Fees differ between providers and change from time to time, so compare them on each provider’s own website before you choose. Also check Shopify’s list of supported gateways for South Africa inside your dashboard, because availability can change and not every provider works in every country.</p>

<p>Remember that a third-party gateway adds an extra Shopify fee on every sale: 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow and 0.6% on Advanced. Include it when you work out your margins.</p>

<h3>Step 5: Set up shipping</h3>
<p>Create shipping zones that match how you deliver. The rates below are examples only. Set yours using what your courier or the post office actually charges you.</p>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Local (your province):</strong> example R50, one to two days</li>
  <li><strong>Rest of South Africa:</strong> example R100, three to five days</li>
  <li><strong>International:</strong> only offer it once you are sure about courier costs and customs</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> Free shipping above a set order value, for example R1,000, can encourage customers to add one more item. Make sure you can afford it first.</p>

<h3>Step 6: Customise your store and launch</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Upload your logo and choose colours that match your brand.</li>
  <li>Add a banner image to your home page.</li>
  <li>Write an <strong>About</strong> page that tells your story.</li>
  <li>Add a <strong>Contact</strong> page with your email and phone number.</li>
  <li>Add your return, privacy and terms policies.</li>
  <li>Place a test order, then remove your store password and go live.</li>
</ol>

<h2>How to get your first visitors</h2>

<figure style="margin: 24px 0px; text-align: center;">
  <img alt="Five free ways to get visitors to your Shopify store: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, blog posts and Facebook groups" src="/images/blog/b983e31631b7.png" style="border-radius: 8px; height: auto; max-width: 100%;" />

<p>A new store has no visitors, so plan your marketing before launch day. These free channels work well for beginners:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>TikTok:</strong> short videos of your products in use, posted regularly</li>
  <li><strong>Instagram Reels and Stories:</strong> clips, customer feedback and behind-the-scenes content</li>
  <li><strong>YouTube Shorts:</strong> quick demos and unboxings with your store link in the description</li>
  <li><strong>Blog posts:</strong> helpful guides around what you sell, such as “how to choose” or “how to use” articles</li>
  <li><strong>Facebook groups:</strong> join local groups, answer questions first and only share your store where the rules allow it</li>
</ul>
